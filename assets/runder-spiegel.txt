Runder-Spiegel construction

Projection defined by the project author, with longitude lambda and latitude phi
in radians:
    x = sin(lambda/2)
    y = tan(phi/2)
The unit Earth maps to the square [-1,1] x [-1,1].

Geometry used by the animation

The Earth is the unit sphere about the origin. The conceptual observer is fixed
on its surface at O=(0,0,1), looking toward a vertical reflective cylinder
x^2+z^2=r^2. The viewer can orbit this construction independently.

A visible mirror point is M=(r*sin(theta),h,r*cos(theta)). Its outward normal
is n=(sin(theta),0,cos(theta)). Reflecting O-M gives the direction from M toward
the Earth: D=2*dot(n,O-M)*n-(O-M). The first forward intersection M+s*D with the
unit sphere supplies the texture coordinates. The ray/sphere quadratic uses a
rationalized positive root to avoid cancellation at the boundary.

Perspective projection from O onto z=0 gives image coordinates
    X = M.x/(1-M.z), Y = h/(1-M.z).
The cylinder's apparent half-width is r/sqrt(1-r^2). Parameterizing the surface
by normalized image width u and normalized height v avoids division by r.

At finite radius the visible reflection covers only part of the Earth: source
points inside the cylinder and beyond its tangent rays cannot reflect from its
visible exterior. The displayed image comes from ray tracing that actual visible
surface, not from squeezing all of the Earth onto a finite cylinder.

The animation has six stages: globe, reveal the radius-0.35 metal cylinder,
transfer the Earth's surface to the cylinder, flatten the wide reflection,
shrink to radius 0.003, and expand into the final square. During the transfer,
each textured patch moves from its
source point on the Earth along its incident reflection path to the corresponding
point on the cylinder. This single textured surface becomes opaque as it travels,
starting at the globe's existing translucency. No separate ghost globe remains.

At radius 0.35 the textured surface moves along the observer's perspective rays
onto z=0. The metal cylinder becomes faint so the planar image remains visible.
Next the cylinder shrinks while the reflection stays on this plane, narrowing
according to its actual perspective width r/sqrt(1-r^2). This stage adds no
horizontal magnification. The cylinder is never unrolled.

During the final horizontal magnification, the remaining optical radius tends
to zero. The limiting expressions are evaluated directly at zero, so the final
geometry is exact and never divides by the vanishing radius.

In the limit, theta=lambda/2 and h=sin(phi)/(1+cos(phi))=tan(phi/2).
Normalized image width u=sin(theta) gives x=sin(lambda/2), while y=h.
The material's metallic highlights are a studio reflection used for legibility;
the Earth's reflected texture uses the geometric construction above.
