Projection equations and reference data

Aitoff: reuses Winkel Tripel's first three construction stages, rescaled to
thirds: globe, halved-longitude hemisphere, azimuthal equidistant disk, ellipse.
https://proj.org/en/stable/operations/projections/aitoff.html

Hammer: same longitude compression and horizontal doubling as Aitoff, using
Lambert azimuthal equal-area instead of azimuthal equidistant disk coordinates.
For d=sqrt(2/(1+cos(latitude)*cos(longitude/2))), final x=2*d*cos(latitude)*
sin(longitude/2), y=d*sin(latitude). No additional rotations are introduced.
https://proj.org/en/stable/operations/projections/hammer.html
https://github.com/d3/d3-geo-projection/blob/main/src/hammer.js

Mollweide: solve 2*theta+sin(2*theta)=pi*sin(latitude), then use
x=(2*sqrt(2)/pi)*longitude*cos(theta), y=sqrt(2)*sin(theta).
Reuses the Mollweide latitude solver in Goode, without its polar y offset.
Stages: globe, latitude rings with final widths/heights, closed rings tangent
to the final plane, unrolled ellipse. Checkpoints at 0, 1/3, 2/3, 1. The third
stage translates each closed ring forward by 1-radius, placing all prime-meridian
points at z=1 without changing ring radii or latitude heights. Only the final
stage cuts the rings behind and decreases their curvature to zero, keeping the
prime-meridian contact points fixed. No ring highlighting or rotation is added.
https://proj.org/en/stable/operations/projections/moll.html

Sinusoidal:
https://proj.org/en/stable/operations/projections/sinu.html

Robinson: published 5-degree width and height table, quadratic interpolation,
and conventional unit-sphere scale factors 0.8487 and 1.3523.
Uses the same four-stage ring construction as Mollweide: reshape, translate
closed rings to contact the final plane at the prime meridian, then unroll from
those fixed contact points. Checkpoints are 0, 1/3, 2/3, 1.
https://proj.org/en/stable/operations/projections/robin.html
https://github.com/d3/d3-geo-projection/blob/main/src/robinson.js

Equal Earth: Savric, Patterson and Jenny (2018), standard polynomial equations.
Uses the same four-stage ring construction as Mollweide and Robinson, with
its own final latitude heights and ring widths. No ring highlighting is added.
https://proj.org/en/stable/operations/projections/eqearth.html
https://github.com/d3/d3-geo/blob/main/src/projection/equalEarth.js

Interrupted Goode homolosine: Sinusoidal/Mollweide composite, transition at
0.7109889596207567 radians, polar y offset 0.0528035274542, land-oriented lobes.
https://proj.org/en/stable/operations/projections/igh.html
https://github.com/d3/d3-geo-projection/blob/main/src/interrupted/homolosine.js

The animated intermediate surfaces are illustrative geometric constructions.
Final projection coordinates follow the definitions above. Surface patches
are split at the Goode lobe boundaries throughout the animation so no triangle
bridges an interruption. The existing texture and annotations remain attached
to geographic coordinates.

Equirectangular (Plate Carree): x = longitude, y = latitude on a unit sphere.
https://proj.org/en/stable/operations/projections/eqc.html

Lambert azimuthal equal-area: centered on the front point (longitude/latitude
0/0), radial distance 2 sin(c/2), where c is angular distance from the center.
The antipode has a separate boundary vertex for each radial direction. It opens
into the disk rim, so no triangles span the singularity.
https://proj.org/en/stable/operations/projections/laea.html

Lee conformal tetrahedral: stereographic face coordinates followed by the
complex integral of (1-z^3)^(-1/2). The implementation evaluates convergent
power series at the center or nearest corner; corner coefficients are generated
from the polynomial recurrence rather than a truncated lookup table. Reference
corner value and formulation: D3's Lee implementation, based on McIlroy.
https://github.com/d3/d3-geo-polygon/blob/main/src/tetrahedralLee.js
https://github.com/d3/d3-geo-polygon#geotetrahedrallee
The four faces form a regular tetrahedron circumscribed about the unit sphere.
Three faces rotate rigidly about the edges of the front face to form the net.

Platonic-solid unfoldings (Tetrahedron, Cube, Octahedron, Dodecahedron, Icosahedron):
These are central/gnomonic projections onto regular tangent face planes, not
Lee's conformal mapping, Dymaxion, or an equal-area polyhedral projection.
For a unit geographic direction d and unit face normal n, the face coordinate
is d / dot(d,n), choosing the outward face with the largest dot product.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedral_map_projection
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnomonic_projection
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedron
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octahedron
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodecahedron
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icosahedron

The first animation stage moves the unit sphere onto a circumscribed regular
solid. The second rotates rigid faces about shared edges in a spanning tree.
Compact, non-overlapping nets are selected offline by find-platonic-nets.mjs;
the tetrahedron uses the triangular star and the cube the familiar cross.
The chosen trees are stored in js/platonic-nets.js, with no layout search in the
render loop. All five have checkpoints at sphere, closed solid, and flat net.

Lambert conformal conic and Albers equal-area conic:
Unit-sphere equations with standard parallels 30°N and 60°N. Both display the
same 0–85°N region so the two conics can be compared on equal terms. Coordinates
are translated vertically to center the complete sector; this does not affect
scale or distortion. The regional extent is stated in both info summaries.
https://proj.org/en/stable/operations/projections/lcc.html
https://proj.org/en/stable/operations/projections/aea.html
https://github.com/d3/d3-geo/blob/main/src/projection/conicConformal.js
https://github.com/d3/d3-geo/blob/main/src/projection/conicEqualArea.js

Conic checkpoints: northern hemisphere → perspective cone → opened cone →
flat sector → exact Lambert/Albers latitude spacing. The intermediate cone is
an illustrative central projection, not a claim that the final conformal or
equal-area projection is perspective. Opening and flattening are developable:
slant distances and parallel arc lengths are unchanged. The last stage changes
spacing along the flat sector to satisfy the final projection's equations.

Winkel Tripel:
https://proj.org/en/stable/operations/projections/wintri.html
https://github.com/d3/d3-geo-projection/blob/main/src/aitoff.js
https://github.com/d3/d3-geo-projection/blob/main/src/winkel3.js
Uses cos(standard parallel) = 2/pi. Checkpoints: globe → longitudes halved →
azimuthal equidistant disk → horizontally doubled Aitoff ellipse →
Winkel Tripel, moving directly halfway toward equirectangular coordinates.
The last three stages are planar; there is no separate rectangle stop.
The final blend is exactly 50/50.

Torus unfolding (illustrative construction, ending in an equirectangular map):
Douglas DeCarlo and Jean Gallier, Topological Evolution of Surfaces (1996),
Figure 2: push two sphere points together, then expand the pinch into a ring.
https://graphicsinterface.org/wp-content/uploads/gi1996-23.pdf
https://proj.org/en/stable/operations/projections/eqc.html

Checkpoints: sphere, poles meeting at the origin (horn torus), ring torus,
horizontal cylinder, rectangle. Sphere-to-horn interpolation keeps the equator
fixed and uses radius cos(latitude)^2 and height sin(latitude)*cos(latitude).
The torus has major radius 3/4 and tube radius 1/2; its tube angle is twice latitude.
The hole opens to radius 1/4, half its original 1/2 radius. Straightening preserves
the smaller major circumference, producing a correspondingly narrower rectangle.
The polar singularity intentionally changes topology. No polar area is cropped.
Opening the hole preserves the torus's orientation. Straightening cuts the
longitude seam and bends only the major ring, without twisting the tube.
Unrolling opens the polar seam at the back symmetrically upward and downward,
keeping the front equator fixed, with no rolling. The final coordinates are
x=(3/4)*longitude, y=latitude, z=1. This is equirectangular spacing with a 3:2
rectangle instead of Plate Carree's 2:1 rectangle. Distortion is measured from
these final coordinates.
